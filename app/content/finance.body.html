What it does

The IFC estimates India's MSME credit gap at ~$397 billion. For exporters the squeeze is brutal: an international buyer wants 60–90 day payment terms, but a small maker can't fund raw material, production and freight while waiting three months for money. So they simply refuse the order — and India loses the export.

Aaziko's Export Finance module dissolves this constraint. Because every Aaziko order is verified — contract documented, production evidenced by Inspection, delivery assured by Order Assurance — it becomes a bankable asset.

Against that asset, Aaziko arranges export factoring (the maker receives up to 90% at shipment), ECGC-backed credit insurance against buyer default, and working-capital lines under the government's own programs: Niryat Protsahan's 2.75% factoring interest subvention and the ₹20,000 crore CGSE collateral-free guarantee.

The schemes exist. What's been missing is a pipeline of verified MSME orders to attach them to. Aaziko is that pipeline — scheme money delivered at the moment of trade, not after months of paperwork.
